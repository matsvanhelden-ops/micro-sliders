Learning microeconomics by moving sliders

Twelve interactive graphs for a principles course (Pindyck & Rubinfeld, chapters 3, 6, 7, 8, 12, 18). Two recurring characters: Antonio, who runs a pizzeria, and Anita, who spends her money on beer and pizza. Each slider has the same colour as the line it moves. Nothing is stored, no accounts, no cookies.

1 · Present right now (nothing to install)

Opens the whole set as a slideshow: arrow keys or clicker to move, sliders work with the mouse. Press Full screen in the bottom bar.

3 · Steps in PowerPoint

PowerPoint for the web (office.com, also on a lecture-hall PC in the browser):

  1. Download the add-in file of the graph you want (button above). It is a small .xml file.
  2. In PowerPoint: Insert → Add-ins → More add-ins (or search “Add-ins” in the top search box).
  3. Tab My add-ins → Manage my add-ins → Upload my add-in → Browse, choose the file, Upload.
  4. The graph appears on the slide. Drag the corners to make it fill the slide, leaving a small margin.
  5. Start the slide show. Drag the sliders; click next to the graph (or use the bottom bar) to go to the next slide.

PowerPoint on Windows or Mac (desktop): the same add-in works, but the “upload” button does not exist there. Either present from PowerPoint for the web, or ask me: I will set it up on your laptop in ten minutes.

Clicker: after you touch a slider, the graph has the keyboard focus. Click once beside the graph (on the slide margin) and the clicker and arrow keys work again. Tested in PowerPoint for the web.
