Week 3 · MRTS formula (ch. 6)

(MPL)(ΔL) + (MPK)(ΔK) = 0

What Antonio gains from extra workers he must lose from fewer ovens, because he stays on the same isoquant. Change the numbers and the balance always ends at zero.

Week 8 · Remedies: the efficient level of emissions (ch. 18)

How much smoke is efficient? MCA = MEC

Cleaning the oven costs money (marginal cost of abatement, falling in emissions); smoke hurts the neighbours (marginal external cost, rising). Set the emission fee. Antonio reduces smoke as long as cleaning is cheaper than the fee. At a fee of 8 the two curves cross: that is the Pigouvian fee from the lecture.

Week 8 · Public goods (ch. 18)

Lamps on the square: add up what everyone is willing to pay

Street lamps are non-rival and non-excludable. Anita, Antonio and the neighbours each have their own willingness to pay per lamp. For a public good you add the curves vertically: everyone enjoys the same lamp. Add neighbours and watch the efficient number of lamps rise, while nobody buys one alone.

Wrap-up

Every graph asks the same question:
what happens if I change one thing?

Antonio stops where MR = MC. Anita stops where MRS = Px/Py. Antonio mixes workers and ovens where MRTS = w/r. Same idea each time: keep going until the next step no longer pays off.
